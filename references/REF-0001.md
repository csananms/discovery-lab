# REF-0001: Microwave-Assisted Vacuum Frying of Durian Chips

**Source type:** External research paper

**Citation:** Thongcharoenpipat, C., and Yamsaengsung, R. (2023). "Microwave-assisted vacuum frying of durian chips: Impact of ripening level on the drying rate, physio-chemical characteristics, and acceptability." *Food and Bioproducts Processing*, 138, 40-52. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fbp.2023.01.001

**Source identity:** DOI `10.1016/j.fbp.2023.01.001`; *Food and Bioproducts Processing* 138 (2023), pages 40-52.

## Source Evidence

- Under the reported 110 C, 8 kPa conditions, the authors report that Day 1 durian at 560 W reached the target final moisture in 300 s, compared with 420 s at 400 W. Reported specific energy consumption was 22.05 MJ/kg at 560 W and 27.12 MJ/kg at 400 W; reported oil content was 5.38% and 6.57%, respectively (Table 1).
- The study used different slice thicknesses for Day 4 (5-6 mm) than Days 1-3 (2-2.5 mm) because Day 4 fruit was too soft for thinner slicing (Methods). This limits comparisons that attribute Day 4 outcomes to ripening alone.

## Author Interpretation

- The authors interpret higher microwave power as increasing internal heating and water-vapour transport, contributing to shorter frying time.
- The authors interpret greater ripeness as increasing sugar and pectin effects that slow water removal and affect product structure.

## Limits

- This record preserves selected evidence only; it is not an exhaustive literature summary.
- The results are specific to the reported cultivar, preparation, equipment, operating conditions, and measurements.
