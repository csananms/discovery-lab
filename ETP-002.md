# Evidence Transfer Protocol

**Protocol ID:** ETP-002

**Version:** 0.1

**Status:** Conceptual

**Date:** 2026-08-06

## Purpose

The Evidence Transfer Protocol defines how a collaborating participant transfers durable evidence into Discovery Lab. It transfers evidence, not conversations.

## Design Goals

- Preserve provenance, confidence, and historical continuity.
- Keep observations, hypotheses, discovery candidates, and open questions distinct.
- Remain minimal and independent of a specific tool or implementation.
- Support scientific review before evidence changes repository knowledge.

## Scope

ETP-002 concerns the conceptual transfer and review of durable evidence.

It does not define an API, schema, network protocol, storage format, or implementation.

## Participants

- A contributing participant provides incoming evidence. A participant may be a scientist, AI system, implementation agent, or future collaborator.
- Discovery Lab evaluates and integrates evidence into the repository.

## Transfer Object

A transfer object is the smallest durable account of incoming evidence needed for review. It identifies the claim or question, its provenance, its confidence, and its relationship to existing laboratory work.

## Evidence Requirements

Incoming evidence should provide, where known:

- A title.
- One classification: Observation, Hypothesis, Discovery Candidate, or Open Question.
- Supporting evidence.
- A confidence level.
- Provenance.
- Related investigations and discoveries.
- Potential architectural consequences.

Unknown provenance or confidence should be recorded as unknown rather than inferred.

## Discovery Lab Responsibilities

Discovery Lab evaluates each transferred item independently. It preserves the stated confidence, distinguishes evidence from interpretation, identifies conflicts with existing records, and records only repository-worthy knowledge.

The laboratory does not treat transfer as validation. It does not convert a hypothesis into a discovery without additional evidence.

## Repository Outputs

After review, a transfer may result in one or more of the following:

- A notebook update.
- An investigation update.
- A discovery update.
- An experiment update.
- An architectural review.
- An open question.

No repository action is also a valid outcome.

## Lifecycle

1. A participant provides durable evidence.
2. Discovery Lab reviews the evidence and its provenance.
3. Each repository-worthy item is classified independently.
4. The laboratory records any supported update, conflict, or open question.
5. The resulting artifacts preserve the evidence boundary for later review.

## Operating Principles

- Preserve provenance.
- Preserve confidence.
- Preserve historical integrity.
- Preserve scientific discipline.
- Prefer repository knowledge over conversational memory.

## Success Criteria

ETP-002 succeeds when a future collaborator can contribute and later understand durable evidence without relying on a specific tool, conversation, or implementation.

## Open Questions

- What minimum provenance is sufficient when the originating session cannot be retained?
- How should a transfer reference an investigation before a dedicated investigation artifact exists?
- When should related records be revised, and when should a transfer only record a confidence boundary?

## Relationship to the Trust Kernel

ETP-002 is not a Trust Kernel and does not establish one. Its concerns with provenance, evidence, confidence, and preservation overlap with the candidate invariants recorded in [EXP-0002](./experiments/EXP-0002/README.md).

Whether a minimal Trust Kernel can support multiple domains remains a proposed experiment. ETP-002 neither validates nor depends on that hypothesis.
