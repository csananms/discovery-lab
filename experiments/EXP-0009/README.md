# EXP-0009: Beef Quality Image Processing Research Intake

**Date:** 2026-08-09

**Status:** Examined

## Research Question

Can Discovery Lab preserve a proposed ImageJ/Fiji research program for beef-quality assessment without treating its planning assumptions, preliminary references, or candidate methods as verified evidence?

## Input

- [REF-0007: Image Processing for Beef Quality Assessment Project Context](../../references/REF-0007.md)

## Evidence Boundary

The incoming source is research planning. It proposes a three-paper trajectory and lists questions for later literature review, method development, validation, and field implementation. It supplies no independently examined literature, images, measurements, reference-method comparison, or operational result.

## Classified Outputs

### ImageJ/Fiji Is Positioned as Conventional Image Analysis

- **Classification:** Observation
- **Supporting evidence:** The source explicitly distinguishes transparent, researcher-controlled digital image processing from artificial intelligence or machine learning.
- **Confidence:** High for the source's stated terminology boundary; not a technical assessment of all ImageJ/Fiji capabilities.
- **Lab reasoning:** Future records should not label a conventional ImageJ workflow as AI solely because it processes images.

### A Research Program Was Proposed in Three Stages

- **Classification:** Observation
- **Supporting evidence:** The source orders a proposed review, ImageJ marbling-method development and validation, then local slaughterhouse implementation assessment.
- **Confidence:** High for the proposed sequence; no feasibility, novelty, or outcome is demonstrated.
- **Lab reasoning:** The sequence remains project context and does not commit Discovery Lab to a manuscript or implementation plan.

### Transparent Image Analysis May Support Accessible Marbling Assessment

- **Classification:** Hypothesis
- **Supporting evidence:** The source proposes standardized image acquisition, parameter recording, reference-method comparison, and reproducibility checks for an accessible ImageJ/Fiji workflow.
- **Confidence:** Initial. The source provides no literature synthesis, validation data, or implementation evidence.
- **Lab reasoning:** Any method claim depends on a genuine beef-specific research gap and a pre-specified validation approach.

### Reproducible Image Analysis Requires Preserved Acquisition and Processing Context

- **Classification:** Discovery Candidate
- **Supporting evidence:** The source identifies lighting, white balance, camera geometry, background, segmentation thresholds, raw images, metadata, exclusions, and failed segmentation as factors that should be documented.
- **Confidence:** Initial. This is a candidate research-practice lesson, not evidence that a specific workflow is reproducible or sufficient.
- **Potential architectural consequence:** None established. Existing references and experiments can preserve the planning boundary without a food-engineering or image-processing foundation.

### Literature Gap and Validation Basis

- **Classification:** Open Question
- **Supporting evidence:** The source identifies uncertainty about existing beef-marbling methods, official-grade comparisons, imaging protocols, repeatability, low-cost operation, slaughterhouse feasibility, and appropriate statistics.
- **Question:** Does a focused beef-specific literature review establish a research gap and validation basis sufficient to justify a later ImageJ/Fiji method study?

## Items Not Transferred

- Any image, sample, carcass, slaughterhouse, operator, or business record.
- Any raw or derived experimental measurement, processing parameter, validation result, or statistic.
- The named preliminary papers as independently verified supporting literature.
- Any claim of novelty, effectiveness, accuracy, reproducibility, or field feasibility.

## Result

The existing Evidence Transfer Protocol can preserve this research-planning context without new architecture. The smallest justified next action, if pursued, is a focused scholarly literature review that independently tests the proposed novelty and validation assumptions before any method-development or field-implementation study begins.
