# EXP-0010: Early AI-Assisted Textbook Development Intake

**Date:** 2026-08-09

**Status:** Examined

## Research Question

Can Discovery Lab preserve an early AI-assisted textbook-development experiment as historical evidence without treating generated educational content or the source's collaboration claims as validated knowledge?

## Input

- [REF-0008: Early AI-Assisted Textbook Development Through Learning by Doing](../../references/REF-0008.md)

## Evidence Boundary

The incoming source is a participant account of an exploratory interaction. It describes process, roles, and perceived limitations but supplies no retained dialogue, generated passages, primary sources, learning outcomes, or independent content review. Laboratory reasoning therefore evaluates what the account can support as historical evidence only.

## Classified Outputs

### Human Direction and AI Drafting Were Distinguished

- **Classification:** Observation
- **Supporting evidence:** The source attributes learning purpose, textbook structure, assessment direction, and continuous-case selection to the human participant; it attributes candidate-content generation and iterative expansion to AI.
- **Confidence:** Moderate. The role distinction is reported by the participant, but detailed interaction records are not transferred.
- **Lab reasoning:** The account supports preserving a high-level human/AI contribution boundary, not claim-level provenance for the textbook material.

### The Historical Account Reports Limits in Generated Material

- **Classification:** Observation
- **Supporting evidence:** The source describes generated material as sometimes generic, repetitive, insufficiently quantitative, weakly connected to primary evidence, and not rigorous enough for direct graduate-level publication.
- **Confidence:** Moderate for the participant's reported experience; no independent quality assessment is supplied.
- **Lab reasoning:** These are reported limitations, not a universal evaluation of AI-generated educational content.

### AI-Assisted Textbook Work May Be More Useful as Iterative Knowledge Development

- **Classification:** Hypothesis
- **Supporting evidence:** The source describes a recurring cycle of human idea, AI draft, human observation, new constraint, refinement, and further observation.
- **Confidence:** Initial. One unretained exploratory account cannot establish effectiveness or generalizability.
- **Lab reasoning:** The hypothesis is about a collaboration process, not automated textbook production.

### Development History May Be Reusable Evidence for Future Collaboration

- **Classification:** Discovery Candidate
- **Supporting evidence:** The source identifies decisions, corrections, cases, and refinements as potentially meaningful alongside the resulting educational artifact.
- **Confidence:** Initial. The actual development history is not transferred, and its future reuse has not been demonstrated.
- **Candidate connection:** The reported cycle resembles Discovery Lab's observation-to-reflection practice, but this is not evidence that Discovery Lab existed in its current form or that the relationship is continuous.
- **Potential architectural consequence:** None established. Existing references and experiments represent the historical account without an education-specific foundation or provenance extension.

### Validation and Provenance of AI-Assisted Educational Material

- **Classification:** Open Question
- **Supporting evidence:** The source leaves source attachment, claim validation, separation of human decisions from AI synthesis, and potential reuse of development history unresolved.
- **Question:** What minimum evidence and review record would allow AI-assisted educational material to become reliable graduate-level teaching knowledge while preserving the human, source, and AI contribution boundaries?

## Protocol Assessment

| Concern | Result | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Passed | The historical experiment has a title, domain, and intake date. |
| Provenance | Passed with limitation | The participant account identifies broad contribution roles; the original interaction and artifacts are not retained. |
| Evidence | Passed | Historical observations, hypothesis, discovery candidate, and open question are separated from generated content. |
| Historical integrity | Passed | The record preserves the account as an early experiment without retroactively presenting it as a validated Discovery Lab method. |

## Items Not Transferred

- Any generated textbook passage, assessment item, rubric, figure concept, source citation, calculation, or technical claim.
- Any original conversation, prompt sequence, or claim-level authorship record.
- Any claim of teaching effectiveness, academic validity, or graduate-level readiness.

## Result

The existing Evidence Transfer Protocol can preserve this historical collaboration account without new architecture. The account supports a bounded candidate connection to Discovery Lab's evidence-oriented practice, while validation, claim-level provenance, and educational quality remain open questions.
