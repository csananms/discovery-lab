## Classified Evidence

### Convenience-Based AI Workflows Can Create Boundary Ambiguity

- **Classification:** Observation
- **Observation:** Convenience-based AI workflows can create ambiguity in identity, authority, ownership, and machine roles once persistence and dependency increase.
- **Supporting provenance:** The supplied migration account reports that organizational/faculty Codex was used across multiple environments and repositories because it was convenient. As AI-OS and Discovery Lab became longer-term infrastructure, the account reports ambiguity involving execution identity, repository authority, machine role, and intellectual ownership. It also reports an SSH identity mismatch, separation of current and legacy working copies, and clarification of personal, faculty/external-domain, repository, identity, and machine-role boundaries.
- **Confidence:** Moderate. This confidence preserves the account's evidentiary limitation: the operational result is supplied but not independently audited.
- **Related records:** Existing Z4 intake records already preserve source-qualified authority and multi-machine concerns; they do not establish this specific convenience-to-boundary transition.

### Accumulated Convenience May Require a Boundary Checkpoint

- **Classification:** Discovery Candidate
- **Candidate:** Accumulating persistence, authority, or dependency may justify an explicit boundary checkpoint before an experimental workflow becomes durable infrastructure.
- **Supporting evidence:** The supplied migration account reports that boundary clarification became necessary after convenience-based use accumulated across identities, repositories, machines, and domains. The account reports that explicit identity, ownership, authority, and role distinctions were then established.
- **Confidence:** Initial. One supplied operational account does not establish generality, a detection method, or a threshold.
- **Potential consequence:** This may guide a future workflow investigation. It does not introduce a checkpoint, signal, scoring system, architecture, or accepted principle.

### Detecting Boundary Risk Before Durable Infrastructure Emerges

- **Classification:** Open Question
- **Question:** Can a lightweight checkpoint detect when convenience has accumulated enough boundary risk to require explicit identity, ownership, and authority review?
- **Supporting evidence:** The supplied migration account reports that explicit boundaries were clarified only after convenience-based use had spread across multiple identities, environments, repositories, and machine roles.
- **Confidence:** Not applicable; unresolved.
