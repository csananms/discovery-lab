# D-0004

**Date:** 2026-08-06

**Confidence:** Initial

**Evidence:**

- A Mission 2 architecture session reduced the Discovery Engine to a proposed minimum operating kernel.
- The same session consistently grouped its concerns around capture, classification, traceability, state, review, and preservation.
- A single intake point and three candidate entity types were identified during the discussion.

**Not yet demonstrated:**

- That the proposed kernel is sufficient for an actual investigation.
- That the three entities can represent all intended laboratory artifacts without loss of meaning.
- That a single Discovery Inbox improves classification or traceability.
- That the Trust Kernel generalizes across domains.

## Observations

- The Discovery Engine was reduced to six proposed capabilities: Capture, Register, Link, State, Review, and Preserve.
- Item, Investigation, and Knowledge were proposed as its only first-class entities.
- New ideas, observations, questions, external sources, and experiments were proposed to enter through one Discovery Inbox before classification.
- Later discoveries may revisit earlier understanding.
- Earlier artifacts should be preserved rather than rewritten.
- The architecture was discussed as a way to support trustworthy, self-improving scientist-AI collaboration.

## Supported Architectural Conclusions

- Mission 2 has identified a minimal conceptual boundary for continued Discovery Engine investigation.
- Preservation of prior artifacts is necessary if later understanding is to remain traceable to earlier evidence.
- The Trust Kernel question remains an experiment, not an accepted laboratory principle.

## Research Hypotheses

- The six capabilities may be sufficient for a minimal Discovery Engine.
- Item, Investigation, and Knowledge may provide a sufficient first-class model for laboratory work.
- A single Discovery Inbox may reduce fragmentation before artifacts are classified.
- A minimal Trust Kernel may support multiple domains while preserving domain-specific meaning.
- This architecture may enable trustworthy, self-improving scientist-AI collaboration.

## Open Questions

- What distinguishes an Item from Knowledge, and when does an Item become Knowledge?
- What are the minimum states and transition evidence for an Investigation?
- What information must the Discovery Inbox retain before classification?
- How are revisions represented without rewriting earlier artifacts?
- Which domain-specific meanings must remain outside a shared Trust Kernel?
- What evidence would show that the collaboration is self-improving rather than merely documented?

## Architectural Consequences

- Future Mission 2 work should test the kernel against a bounded investigation before adding structure.
- Artifact history should preserve superseded understanding alongside later conclusions.
- Cross-domain reuse claims should be evaluated through experiments rather than embedded as architecture.

## Possible Experiments

- Run one bounded investigation through Capture, Register, Link, State, Review, and Preserve.
- Classify a representative set of laboratory artifacts as Item, Investigation, or Knowledge and record exceptions.
- Route several artifact types through a single inbox and evaluate traceability after classification.
- Compare a shared Trust Kernel with two domain-specific models to identify what is shared and what must remain local.
